# Minecraft Silhouette and Shape Language

## Purpose
Make the asset recognizable before texture, shading or micro-detail.

## Trigger
Use for creature/character/prop concept design, blockout, reference reconstruction and silhouette critique.

## Inputs
Subject and role, target scale, reference views, emotional/gameplay cues, style card and format constraints.

## Workflow
1. Write a one-sentence recognition test: what makes this subject identifiable?
2. Reduce the subject to 2-5 major masses.
3. Draw or render a solid black silhouette at intended in-game size.
4. Check negative spaces between limbs, ears, horns, tools and body masses.
5. Exaggerate only features that improve recognition or communicate role.
6. Establish proportion ratios and landmark positions before adding detail.
7. Convert the silhouette into simple voxel/Blockbench masses.
8. Inspect front, side, rear and three-quarter views; correct tangencies and merged forms.

## Rules
- Avoid relying on color or tiny texture marks to rescue a weak silhouette.
- Keep pose and appendages readable at gameplay distance.
- Use consistent shape cues: round for soft/friendly, angular for sharp/dangerous, broad for heavy, narrow for agile, unless the art direction says otherwise.
- Use rotated elements/planes only when they improve the form and suit the target format.

## Outputs
Silhouette sheet, proportion guide, shape-language notes, blockout and mismatch list.

## Safety
Do not overwrite the reference or original project during blockout.

## Failure handling
If a single view hides critical structure, mark it unknown and request an additional view rather than inventing certainty.

## Acceptance criteria
The subject can be identified from a flat silhouette at intended in-game size and remains readable from required angles.
